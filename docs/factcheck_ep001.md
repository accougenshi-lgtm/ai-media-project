# EP001 fact check: What if the Moon were twice as close?
| Claim | Basis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Mean Earth–Moon distance 384,400 km | NASA Moon fact sheet | standard value |
| Tidal force ∝ 1/d³ → 8× | Differential gravity: d/dr(GM/r²) ∝ M/r³ | derived |
| 2× wider, 4× sky area, ~4× brighter full moon | angular diameter ∝ 1/d; same albedo/phase → flux ∝ solid angle | derived ("roughly") |
| Orbit under 10 days (9.66 d sidereal) | Kepler III: T ∝ a^1.5; 27.32/2^1.5 | derived |
| No more annular eclipses | Moon ~0.52° → ~1.04°, always > Sun's ~0.53° max | derived |
| Spin-down 64× faster | Tidal torque ∝ k₂M²R⁵/d⁶ (Darwin/MacDonald tidal theory) | derived, simplified (ignores ocean resonance changes) |
| Moon survives | Roche limit ~9,500–18,000 km ≪ 192,200 km | standard |
Not claimed (deliberately): exact tide heights in metres (ocean resonance makes it non-linear).
