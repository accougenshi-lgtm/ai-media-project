## 0. The single most important fact

**YouTube's Partner Program entry bar doubles on 1 Feb 2027** (official help page:
support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009): new creators will need **1,000 subs + 8,000 watch hours / 365 days**
or **1,000 subs + 20M Shorts views / 90 days**. Existing partners are grandfathered.

Today's bar is 1,000 subs + 4,000 hours (or 10M Shorts views in 90 days). **We have 71 days.**

Deadline math (*estimate*): 4,000 h = 240,000 minutes. A 15-min long-form video at ~40% retention = ~6 min watched per view
→ **~40,000 long-form views** total, plus 1,000 subs. Shorts views do **not** count toward watch hours.

Verdict: achievable only if ~1 long-form video breaks out (~30K+ views). Plausible in our top niche (median this-year
search result is ~250K views) but **not likely** for a brand-new channel. We go for it anyway, because the downside
of missing is only "same work, higher bar". Strategy implication: **long-form is the priority; Shorts are for discovery and subs.**

## 5. Gaps we can exploit (hypotheses to test, not facts)
1. **Show the math on screen.** Our test: viewers trust and share "here's *why* it's 64×" more than an asserted number. Doubles as originality proof for YPP review.
2. **Physics-accurate original motion graphics** instead of stock/AI-slop images: zero copyright exposure, recognizable look.
3. **Counter-intuitive *small* changes** ("Moon twice as close", "Earth 1% faster") instead of only cataclysms. Less saturated, endless supply.
4. For gaming: most patch coverage is opinion; **DPS/TTK math tables per patch** are rarer and evergreen-until-next-patch.

## 6. Recommendation: Channel #1 = science what-ifs with real math
**Why:** highest measured demand, small channels break out (The Last Day), evergreen (videos earn for years), and
production is 100% free with what's already built (pilot rendered for $0). Long-form 12–20 min → watch hours.
**What could make it fail:** big incumbents own the broad topics; free-TTS narration may feel flat; our motion-graphic style
might read as "cheap" vs cinematic competitors; one wrong physics claim kills credibility in comments.

## 7. Three channel identities
1. **"Order of Magnitude"**: calm, precise, "one change, real math". Amber-on-space palette (pilot style). *Risk:* too nerdy for broad click-through.
2. **"Scale Shift"**: punchier, bigger stakes, "what breaks first?". *Risk:* drifts toward generic cataclysm content.
3. **"The Sixth Power"**: brand built around surprising exponents (cube laws, inverse squares). *Risk:* narrow name, hard to extend.
→ Default: **Order of Magnitude** (check name availability before creating the channel).

## 8. Three recurring formats
1. **"One Change"** (long-form, 12–18 min): change one constant, follow consequences in order of time (seconds → millennia).
2. **"The Number"** (Shorts, 45–75 s): one surprising number, derived on screen (pilot EP001 is this format).
3. **"Ranked by Physics"** (long-form, 15–20 min): e.g. "Every planet ranked by how fast it would kill you", data-backed tier list.

## 12. Pipeline
idea → check demand in research.db → fact sheet with sources → script → `episodes/epNNN.py` → render (~4 min/min of video) →

Note: videos uploaded via the API from an unaudited project are locked to private, so publishing stays a manual click (fine; it is also your QA gate).
