# Fact-check EP008 "A Nearby Star Is Orbiting the Galaxy Backwards ⭐" (episodes/ep008_backwards_star.json), checked 2026-11-26
Idea #22 (motion theme; @cosmoinmotion_001 "A star 13 light-years from us is going the wrong way around the Milky Way", 387K).
Wikipedia/kencroswell.com were not reachable from this server (DNS block); facts below come from papers/pages returned by web search on 2026-11-26.

| Claim | Source / derivation |
|---|---|
| Kapteyn's Star, ~13 (12.83) light-years away; closest halo star to the Solar System | Wikipedia summary as returned by search ("red subdwarf about 12.83 light-years from Earth... closest halo star"); Guinan et al. 2016, arXiv:1602.01912 |
| Orbits the galaxy retrograde ("the wrong way", "backwards") | Guinan et al. 2016 (arXiv:1602.01912): "one of the relatively few well-established retrograde Galactic orbits" in the solar neighbourhood. Derivation: Kotoneva et al. 2005 (A&A, aa2287-04) give V ≈ −288 km/s relative to the Sun; the Sun circles at ≈ +230–250 km/s, so the star's own rotation ≈ −40 to −60 km/s (negative = opposite direction) |
| Almost every nearby star, Sun included, circles the same way | Galactic disc stars co-rotate (standard); halo stars like this are a small minority near the Sun |
| Sun: one lap ≈ 230 million years | NASA StarChild (also used in EP007) |
| Passes us at ~290 km/s; header ≈ 293 km/s | space velocity relative to Sun ν = 293 km/s (paper returned by search, arXiv:1811.08902/2109.10912 context); U,V,W ≈ +21, −288, −53 km/s (Kotoneva 2005) → √(21²+288²+53²) = 294 km/s |
| Earth to Moon in 22 minutes | 384,400 km / 293 km/s = 1,312 s = 21.9 min |
| 8.6″ per year, 2nd-highest proper motion after Barnard's Star | 8,644 mas/yr (Gaia-based paper, arXiv:1811.08902); "second only to that of Barnard's Star" (Wikipedia via search). Until 1916 it was the largest known (Kapteyn 1897) |
| Moves a full-Moon width in ~2 centuries | Moon's apparent diameter ≈ 31′ = 1,860″ (NASA Moon fact sheet: 0.49–0.55°); 1,860 / 8.64 = 215 years |
| Low metal content says it is ancient | StarDate: "lower proportion of heavier elements suggests great age"; composition + motion = halo population |
| One theory: born in a smaller galaxy the Milky Way swallowed (Omega Centauri = suspected core) | Wylie-de Boer et al. 2010 (Kapteyn moving group chemically like ω Cen); contested by arXiv:1506.02041 (no resemblance). Script says "one theory", header says "debated" |
