# Fact-check EP007 "You're Moving 1.3 Million km/h and Can't Feel It" (episodes/ep007_you_are_moving.json), checked 2026-11-26
Idea #23 (motion theme, from @cosmoinmotion_001 study). Original working title said "2 million km/h": that figure is the **Local Group's** speed
vs the CMB (620 ± 15 km/s = 2.23M km/h), not yours, so the script uses the Sun's/your speed instead. Speeds are vectors: the script never adds them up.

| Claim | Source / derivation |
|---|---|
| You move ~1.3 million km/h vs the Big Bang's afterglow (CMB) | Planck 2018 (Planck Collaboration I, A&A 641, A1, 2020): solar dipole velocity 369.82 ± 0.11 km/s; × 3,600 = 1,331,000 km/h. Earth's orbital motion changes this by ±30 km/s over the year (±8%), so "1.3 million" holds all year |
| ≈ 1,500× a passenger jet | cruise ≈ 900 km/h; 1,331,000 / 900 = 1,479 |
| Spin: almost 1,700 km/h at the equator; header 1,670 km/h | equatorial circumference 40,075 km / sidereal day 23.934 h = 1,674 km/h (NASA Earth fact sheet values) |
| ≈ 1.4× the speed of sound | sea-level sound 1,235 km/h (343 m/s at 20 °C); 1,674/1,235 = 1.36 |
| Orbit: 107,000 km/h | NASA Earth fact sheet mean orbital velocity 29.78 km/s × 3,600 = 107,208 km/h https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html |
| London to New York in ~3 minutes | great-circle ≈ 5,570 km / 29.78 km/s = 187 s = 3.1 min |
| Sun around the galaxy at over 800,000 km/h; one lap ≈ 230 million years | NASA StarChild: 828,000 km/h, ~230 Myr https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question18.html ; recent estimates 220–239 km/s (792,000–860,000 km/h), so the script says "about eight hundred thousand" |
| You feel acceleration, not constant velocity | Newton's 1st law / equivalence principle (standard physics) |
| Bend of Earth's orbit = 0.06% of g | a = v²/r = (29,780 m/s)² / 1.496e11 m = 0.00593 m/s²; / 9.81 = 0.060% |
| ≈ a golf ball's weight on a 70 kg person | 70 kg × 0.0006 = 42 g; golf ball max 45.93 g (R&A/USGA rules) |
| Earth falls along with you (so you don't feel even that) | free fall: you and Earth are both in orbit around the Sun (equivalence principle); only tiny tidal effects remain |
